# An Empirical Examination of the Relationship Between Capability Maturity and Firm Performance Across Manufacturing and IT Industries

This repository contains supporting materials and analysis for the paper *“An Empirical Examination of the Relationship Between Capability Maturity and Firm Performance Across Manufacturing and IT Industries”* published in *Management and Production Engineering Review*.

## Paper

- **Title:** An Empirical Examination of the Relationship Between Capability Maturity and Firm Performance Across Manufacturing and IT Industries  
- **Authors:** L. Hayes, J. Lu, & D. Rezania  
- **Journal:** *Management and Production Engineering Review*, Vol. 13 (2022)  
- **DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.24425/mper.2022.142055](https://doi.org/10.24425/mper.2022.142055)  
- **Ranking:** SJR Q3

## In Plain Language

This study explores whether firms with more mature management capabilities, such as standardized processes, performance tracking, and continuous improvement—achieve stronger performance outcomes.  
Drawing on survey and performance data from both manufacturing and IT firms, we find that higher capability maturity correlates with better efficiency and profitability.  
However, the strength of this relationship differs across industries, suggesting that capability maturity contributes to performance in context-dependent ways.

## Paper Highlight: 

| # | Why it matters | What the paper delivers |
|---|---------------|-------------------------|
| **1** | **External vs internal motives.**  Certification can be costly; boards want to know *why* firms seek it. | Finds that the **likelihood of a CMMI appraisal rises sharply with the share of government sales and with firm size**, signalling that many appraisals are contract-driven rather than process-driven. |
| **2** | **Profit payoff depends on industry.**  Prior studies mixed all sectors together. | Shows **CMMI boosts profitability in manufacturing but *reduces* it in IT**; industry moderates the maturity-performance link. |
| **3** | **Largest public-data test to date.** Previous maturity research relied on small surveys. | Integrates SEC financials with the CMMI-PARS database for **2,061 firm-years (2017-18)** across both industries, creating a replicable open dataset. |
| **4** | **Methodological rigour.**  Critiques often cite endogeneity. | Combines **logistic regression, OLS with interaction terms, and entropy balancing** to verify results under multiple specifications.|
| **5** | **Contract-driven certification may mask true capability.** | Evidence suggests many firms pursue CMMI primarily to access government contracts, raising questions about the strategic value of “check-box” maturity. |

### Managerial Takeaways

* **Know your motive.**  If certification is *only* to win government work, expect limited internal efficiency gains—especially in IT.  
* **Manufacturers reap financial returns; IT firms may not.**  Weigh the cost–benefit of CMMI differently by sector. 
* **Size (and bureaucracy) matter.**  Larger firms are more likely to succeed in certification and to capture its contractual benefits. 
* **Pair maturity models with genuine process improvement.**  Contract-driven, minimalist appraisals risk “limited internal performance improvements.” 
* **Consider alternative ROI metrics.**  For sectors with negative profit effects, track non-financial wins (risk reduction, contract access) to justify the spend.
